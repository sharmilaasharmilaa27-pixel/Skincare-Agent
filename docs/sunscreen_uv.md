# Sunscreen and UV Protection

## Introduction

Sunscreen is one of the most important steps in any skincare routine. It protects against ultraviolet (UV) radiation from the sun, which causes sunburn, premature aging, hyperpigmentation, and skin cancer. Daily sunscreen use is essential regardless of weather or season.

## UV Radiation: UVA vs UVB

### UVA Rays

UVA rays have a longer wavelength (320-400 nm) and penetrate deep into the dermis. They are present year-round, pass through glass, and are primarily responsible for photoaging (wrinkles, loss of elasticity) and hyperpigmentation. UVA rays account for up to 95% of the UV radiation reaching the Earth's surface.

### UVB Rays

UVB rays have a shorter wavelength (280-320 nm) and primarily affect the epidermis. They are the main cause of sunburn and play a key role in skin cancer development. UVB intensity varies by season, time of day, and geographic location.

### Broad Spectrum

Broad-spectrum sunscreen protects against both UVA and UVB rays. Look for products labeled "broad spectrum" on the packaging.

## SPF Explained

SPF (Sun Protection Factor) measures protection against UVB rays specifically:

- **SPF 15** blocks about 93% of UVB rays
- **SPF 30** blocks about 97% of UVB rays
- **SPF 50** blocks about 98% of UVB rays

Dermatologists recommend SPF 30 or higher for daily use. Higher SPF values offer marginally better protection but must be reapplied regularly.

## Types of Sunscreen

### Chemical Sunscreens

Chemical sunscreens absorb UV radiation and convert it to heat. Common active ingredients include avobenzone, oxybenzone, octisalate, octocrylene, homosalate, and octinoxate. They tend to be lightweight and cosmetically elegant.

### Mineral (Physical) Sunscreens

Mineral sunscreens sit on top of the skin and reflect UV rays. Active ingredients include zinc oxide and titanium dioxide. They are gentle on sensitive skin, provide immediate protection upon application, and are less likely to cause irritation.

## Application Guidelines

1. **Amount:** Use approximately 1/4 teaspoon (1.2 mL) for the face and neck.
2. **Timing:** Apply 15-30 minutes before sun exposure.
3. **Reapplication:** Reapply every 2 hours, or immediately after swimming or sweating.
4. **Daily use:** Apply sunscreen every morning as the final step in your skincare routine, even on cloudy days.

## Sunscreen and Hyperpigmentation

Sunscreen prevents and treats hyperpigmentation, dark spots, and melasma. UV exposure triggers melanin production, worsening existing pigmentation. Daily sunscreen use combined with brightening ingredients like vitamin C and niacinamide provides the best results.

## Photoaging Prevention

Consistent sunscreen use prevents premature aging signs including wrinkles, fine lines, leathery texture, and age spots. Studies show that daily sunscreen users show 24% less skin aging compared to intermittent users over 4 years.

## Key Takeaways

- Use broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher every day.
- Reapply every 2 hours when outdoors.
- Mineral sunscreens are ideal for sensitive skin.
- Sunscreen is essential for preventing hyperpigmentation and photoaging.
- No sunscreen blocks 100% of UV rays — combine with hats and shade.
