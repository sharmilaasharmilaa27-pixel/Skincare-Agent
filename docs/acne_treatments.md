# Acne Treatments

## Introduction

Acne is a common skin condition that occurs when hair follicles become clogged with oil and dead skin cells. It typically appears on the face, forehead, chest, upper back, and shoulders. Acne can be persistent and can cause emotional distress, but effective treatments are available.

## Types of Acne

### Comedonal Acne

Comedonal acne is characterized by non-inflammatory lesions called comedones. Blackheads (open comedones) and whiteheads (closed comedones) are the most common form. This type of acne occurs when pores clog with oil and dead skin cells.

### Inflammatory Acne

Inflammatory acne includes papules, pustules, nodules, and cysts. These lesions are red, swollen, and often painful. They occur when bacteria infect the clogged pores, leading to inflammation.

### Severe Acne

Severe acne consists of large, deep nodules and cysts that can cause scarring. This type often requires prescription-strength treatments and dermatologist care.

## Key Ingredients for Acne

### Salicylic Acid

Salicylic acid is a beta-hydroxy acid (BHA) that penetrates pores to dissolve oil and dead skin cells. It is oil-soluble, making it effective for treating clogged pores and blackheads. Salicylic acid also has anti-inflammatory properties that reduce redness and swelling.

Common concentrations in over-the-counter products range from 0.5% to 2%.

### Benzoyl Peroxide

Benzoyl peroxide kills acne-causing bacteria (Cutibacterium acnes) and helps reduce inflammation. It also has mild comedolytic properties that help unclog pores. Concentrations range from 2.5% to 10%, with 2.5% being as effective as higher concentrations with fewer side effects.

### Niacinamide

Niacinamide (vitamin B3) regulates oil production, reduces inflammation, and improves skin barrier function. It also helps fade post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation. Niacinamide is well-tolerated by most skin types and can be used alongside other acne treatments.

### Retinol

Retinol accelerates skin cell turnover, preventing pores from becoming clogged. It also reduces inflammation and helps fade acne scars over time. Start with a low concentration (0.25% to 0.5%) and gradually increase to avoid irritation.

## Acne Myths

- **Myth:** Acne is caused by poor hygiene. **Truth:** Acne is primarily driven by hormones, genetics, and oil production, not surface cleanliness.
- **Myth:** You should pop pimples to get rid of them. **Truth:** Popping can push bacteria deeper and cause scarring.
- **Myth:** Sunscreen causes acne. **Truth:** Non-comedogenic sunscreen can actually help prevent acne-related hyperpigmentation.

## Daily Acne Routine

1. **Cleanse** with a gentle, non-comedogenic cleanser twice daily.
2. **Treat** with salicylic acid or benzoyl peroxide.
3. **Moisturize** with a lightweight, oil-free moisturizer.
4. **Protect** with broad-spectrum sunscreen every morning.

## When to See a Dermatologist

See a dermatologist if over-the-counter treatments do not work after 8 to 12 weeks, if acne is severe or cystic, or if acne is causing scarring or significant emotional distress. Prescription treatments include topical retinoids, antibiotics, oral contraceptives, and isotretinoin.

## Key Takeaways

- Salicylic acid and benzoyl peroxide are effective over-the-counter treatments.
- Niacinamide and retinol help with oil control and scarring.
- Consistency and patience are key — acne treatments take weeks to show results.
- Always wear sunscreen to prevent post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation.
- Severe acne requires professional dermatological care.
