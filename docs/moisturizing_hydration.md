# Moisturizing and Hydration

## Introduction

Moisturizing is essential for maintaining healthy skin barrier function and preventing dryness, flaking, and irritation. Proper hydration keeps skin plump, smooth, and resilient against environmental stressors. Everyone needs a moisturizer, regardless of skin type.

## Skin Barrier Function

The skin barrier (stratum corneum) is the outermost layer of skin that protects against water loss and environmental aggressors. A compromised barrier leads to dryness, sensitivity, increased transepidermal water loss (TEWL), and susceptibility to irritation. Moisturizers support barrier repair and maintenance.

## Humectants

Humectants attract water to the skin surface and draw moisture from the environment and deeper skin layers.

### Hyaluronic Acid

Hyaluronic acid (HA) is a powerful humectant that can hold up to 1000 times its weight in water. It exists naturally in the skin and decreases with age. Low-molecular-weight HA penetrates deeper, while high-molecular-weight HA sits on the surface for immediate plumping. HA works best in humid environments and on damp skin.

### Glycerin

Glycerin is a gentle, well-tolerated humectant found in most moisturizers. It draws moisture into the skin and supports barrier function. Glycerin is suitable for all skin types and is often paired with occlusives to lock in hydration.

### Beta-Glucan

Beta-glucan is a natural polysaccharide that deeply hydrates and soothes the skin. It also has immune-modulating and wound-healing properties. It is particularly beneficial for sensitive and irritated skin.

## Emollients

Emollients fill in gaps between skin cells, smoothing rough texture and improving skin flexibility. Common emollients include squalane, jojoba oil, shea butter, and various plant oils. They make the skin feel soft and supple.

### Squalane

Squalane is a lightweight, non-comedogenic oil that mimics the skin's natural sebum. It provides deep hydration without clogging pores. It is suitable for all skin types, including oily and acne-prone skin.

## Occlusives

Occlusives form a protective film on the skin surface to prevent water evaporation. Common occlusives include petrolatum, dimethicone, beeswax, and mineral oil. They are particularly effective in dry climates and for very dry skin types.

### Dimethicone

Dimethicone is a silicone-based occlusive that creates a breathable barrier. It also smooths the skin texture and enhances the delivery of other skincare ingredients. It is non-irritating and well-tolerated.

## Moisturizer Selection by Skin Type

### Oily Skin

Use lightweight, oil-free, gel-based moisturizers. Look for hyaluronic acid, glycerin, and squalane. Avoid heavy occlusives and comedogenic ingredients.

### Dry Skin

Use richer, cream-based moisturizers with ceramides, fatty acids, petrolatum, and dimethicone. Apply immediately after cleansing on damp skin.

### Combination Skin

Use lightweight lotions or gels for the T-zone and richer creams for dry areas. Or use a balanced moisturizer that works across all zones.

### Sensitive Skin

Use fragrance-free, hypoallergenic moisturizers with ceramides, centella asiatica, and niacinamide. Avoid alcohol, essential oils, and strong acids.

## When to Moisturize

Apply moisturizer twice daily — morning and night. Always apply to damp skin to lock in hydration. In dry environments, consider layering a hydrating serum under a richer cream.

## Key Takeaways

- Hydration is essential for all skin types.
- Humectants attract water, emollients smooth, occlusives lock in moisture.
- Hyaluronic acid and glycerin are key humectants.
- Ceramides support skin barrier repair.
- Apply moisturizer to damp skin for best results.
