# Skincare Ingredients Reference

## Introduction

This reference covers the most important skincare ingredients, their mechanisms, benefits, ideal skin types, and usage guidelines. Use this as a quick lookup for ingredient information and compatibility guidance.

## Ingredient Database

### Niacinamide (Vitamin B3)

- **Mechanism:** Regulates sebum production, strengthens skin barrier, reduces inflammation, inhibits melanin transfer
- **Benefits:** Controls oil, fades dark spots, reduces redness, improves barrier function, enhances skin elasticity
- **Ideal skin types:** All skin types, especially oily, acne-prone, and sensitive skin
- **Concentration:** 2% to 10% (2-5% for sensitive skin, 5-10% for normal/oily)
- **Compatible with:** Retinol, vitamin C, hyaluronic acid, ceramides, peptides, AHAs/BHAs
- **Not compatible with:** None significant at normal pH ranges
- **Usage:** Daily, morning and night. Apply after cleansing, before moisturizer.
- **Side effects:** Rare; mild flushing at high concentrations initially

### Hyaluronic Acid

- **Mechanism:** Powerful humectant that binds up to 1000x its weight in water
- **Benefits:** Deep hydration, plumping effect, improves skin texture, reduces appearance of fine lines
- **Ideal skin types:** All skin types, especially dry and dehydrated skin
- **Concentration:** 0.5% to 2% in serums; higher in moisturizers
- **Compatible with:** All ingredients including retinoids, vitamin C, niacinamide, ceramides
- **Not compatible with:** None; however, in very dry environments it can pull moisture from skin without occlusive sealing
- **Usage:** Apply to damp skin. Layer under moisturizer to lock in hydration.
- **Side effects:** None significant; can feel tacky if over-applied

### Retinol

- **Mechanism:** Vitamin A derivative that accelerates cell turnover, stimulates collagen production, normalizes desquamation
- **Benefits:** Reduces fine lines and wrinkles, treats acne, fades hyperpigmentation, improves skin texture
- **Ideal skin types:** Normal to oily skin; sensitive skin should start low
- **Concentration:** OTC 0.25% to 0.5%; prescription 0.025% to 0.1% (tretinoin)
- **Compatible with:** Niacinamide, ceramides, hyaluronic acid, moisturizer
- **Not compatible with:** Strong acids (AHA/BHA) should not be used simultaneously; benzoyl peroxide may deactivate retinoids
- **Usage:** Start 2x per week on alternating nights, gradually increase. PM only. Always use SPF the next morning.
- **Side effects:** Dryness, peeling, redness, irritation (retinoid purge lasting 4-6 weeks)

### Salicylic Acid (BHA)

- **Mechanism:** Oil-soluble beta-hydroxy acid that penetrates pores, dissolves excess oil and dead skin cells
- **Benefits:** Treats and prevents acne, reduces blackheads and whiteheads, exfoliates surface, anti-inflammatory
- **Ideal skin types:** Oily, acne-prone, combination skin
- **Concentration:** 0.5% to 2% in OTC products
- **Compatible with:** Niacinamide, moisturizer, ceramides (to counter dryness)
- **Not compatible with:** Strong retinoids at same time; avoid combining with other strong acids
- **Usage:** 1-2x daily for acne treatment. Apply after cleansing, before moisturizer.
- **Side effects:** Dryness, peeling, mild irritation with overuse

### Benzoyl Peroxide

- **Mechanism:** Kills Cutibacterium acnes bacteria, reduces inflammation, has mild comedolytic properties
- **Benefits:** Treats inflammatory acne, prevents new breakouts, reduces redness
- **Ideal skin types:** Acne-prone skin, especially inflammatory acne
- **Concentration:** 2.5% to 10% (2.5% is as effective as 10% with fewer side effects)
- **Compatible with:** Retinoids (use at different times of day), niacinamide, moisturizer
- **Not compatible with:** Tretinoin (use benzoyl peroxide in AM and retinoid in PM); bleaches fabrics
- **Usage:** Apply thin layer to affected areas after cleansing. Start every other day.
- **Side effects:** Dryness, peeling, redness, bleaching of clothes and towels

### Vitamin C (L-Ascorbic Acid)

- **Mechanism:** Potent antioxidant that neutralizes free radicals, inhibits melanin synthesis, co-factor for collagen production
- **Benefits:** Brightens skin, fades hyperpigmentation, protects against UV/sun damage, boosts collagen
- **Ideal skin types:** All skin types, especially dull, uneven tone, or sun-damaged skin
- **Concentration:** 10% to 20% L-ascorbic acid for effectiveness
- **Compatible with:** Vitamin E, ferulic acid (stabilizing synergy), niacinamide, sunscreen, hyaluronic acid
- **Not compatible with:** None significant at proper pH (below 3.5)
- **Usage:** AM routine after cleansing, before moisturizer and sunscreen. Store in opaque, air-tight container.
- **Side effects:** Mild tingling at higher concentrations; irritation at concentrations above 20%

### Ceramides

- **Mechanism:** Lipid molecules that form the protective lipid matrix of the stratum corneum
- **Benefits:** Restore skin barrier, prevent transepidermal water loss (TEWL), reduce sensitivity, improve hydration
- **Ideal skin types:** All skin types, especially dry, sensitive, eczema-prone, and barrier-compromised skin
- **Concentration:** Included in moisturizers at effective barrier-repair levels
- **Compatible with:** All ingredients; best paired with cholesterol and fatty acids in 1:1:1 ratio
- **Not compatible with:** None; essential supportive ingredient for virtually all routines
- **Usage:** Daily, morning and night as part of moisturizer routine.
- **Side effects:** None; extremely well-tolerated

### Azelaic Acid

- **Mechanism:** Naturally occurring dicarboxylic acid that inhibits tyrosinase, has anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial properties
- **Benefits:** Treats acne, reduces redness and inflammation, fades hyperpigmentation, gentle exfoliation
- **Ideal skin types:** Sensitive, acne-prone, rosacea-prone skin; suitable for all skin types
- **Concentration:** 10% OTC, 15% to 20% prescription
- **Compatible with:** Niacinamide, retinol (at different times), ceramides, sunscreen
- **Not compatible with:** Strong acids should be spaced out
- **Usage:** 1-2x daily after cleansing. Can be used long-term.
- **Side effects:** Mild tingling or itching initially; well-tolerated overall

### Peptides

- **Mechanism:** Short chains of amino acids that signal fibroblasts to produce collagen and elastin
- **Benefits:** Improve skin firmness, reduce fine lines, support wound healing, enhance skin elasticity
- **Ideal skin types:** All skin types, especially aging, mature, or thinning skin
- **Concentration:** Varies by peptide type; typically 1% to 10% in formulations
- **Compatible with:** Vitamin C, niacinamide, retinol, ceramides, hyaluronic acid, sunscreen
- **Not compatible with:** None significant
- **Usage:** Daily in serum or moisturizer step. AM and PM.
- **Side effects:** None significant; extremely gentle

### Squalane

- **Mechanism:** Lightweight oil that mimics skin's natural sebum; emollient and antioxidant
- **Benefits:** Deep hydration without clogging pores, antioxidant protection, improves skin texture, balances oil production
- **Ideal skin types:** All skin types, especially oily, combination, and acne-prone skin
- **Concentration:** 1% to 100% in serums and moisturizers
- **Compatible with:** All ingredients; excellent carrier for other active ingredients
- **Not compatible with:** None
- **Usage:** 2-3 drops after serum, before moisturizer. AM and PM.
- **Side effects:** None; non-comedogenic and highly tolerable
